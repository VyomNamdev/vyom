## 3. Positioning (Section 4)
One-line promise: "Real cases, told like a film you can't stop watching." Animated, long-form retellings of famous real crimes,
disappearances and documented "haunted" cases, built for binge-watching. Visually: Archive in Minutes' illustrated mode applied to crime.

### Case selection rules
- Famous and searchable (Wikipedia coverage, documentaries or major news).
- A built-in mystery or twist (unsolved, wrong suspect, double life, betrayal).
- Enough documented facts (court records, reputable news, books) to fill 25 min without inventing anything.
- Resolved legally, or carefully framed: only what sources say, "alleged", never accuse a living person.
- Older cases first. Haunted cases = documented events; paranormal claims as claims ("residents reported").
- **Avoid:** live ongoing trials as main topics; very recent cases involving children. Never show a child as a victim in a thumbnail, never depict violence against children; consider skipping cases where that is the main subject (e.g. Watts).

Recurring series: "Solved After 40 Years" (DNA / genetic genealogy). Indian cases ~30%. Disappearances caught on camera (clue-first).
Haunted places with real crimes: plan 4–6 for Sept–Oct 2027. AI-era crimes: 10% trend slot, concluded cases only. Doc tie-ins within 1–2 weeks of a streaming release.

## 5. Hook rules (Section D) — every video, no exceptions
**D3 crime rules**
1. Start inside the story: the clue, the contradiction or the last normal moment. Never "today we're talking about".
2. A time and place by second 15 ("June 10th, 1912. Villisca, Iowa.").
3. Say the central question out loud by second 30.
4. Title and thumbnail built on the twist or object, not just the victim's name.
5. The clue from the hook must pay off near the end.
6. No gore, blood or bodies in the first 30 seconds or the thumbnail.
7. Re-hook every 30–90 seconds (new clue, new question, "but…").
8. Calm narrator voice; let silence do the scaring.

**D4 scorecards — ship only 8/10 or higher**
- Hook (0/1/2): Stakes (life-or-death mystery) • Specificity • Speed • Curiosity gap • Truth and match.
- Title (2 each): proven formula • searched topic/famous name • number/twist/concrete detail • under ~60 chars • honest.
- Thumbnail (2 each): readable at 10% • one character or one object • ≤3 words • matches title promise without repeating • no gore.

**D5 templates:** "[Date]. [Place]. [Last normal moment]. By morning, [shocking change]." • "The [object] was the first clue. It took police [time] to understand what it meant." • "Everyone in [town] said [person] was [trusted role]. They were wrong." • "The doors were locked from the inside. All of them." • "Today you can [visit/book] the place where it happened. Most people don't stay the night."
**Hook bank:** last normal moment • impossible detail • betrayal • question • aftermath first.
**What works (D2):** betrayal by someone close; one strange object/detail over the victim's name; clue-first puzzle (clue → rewind → suspects → payoff); long, calm, binge-able (20–45 min); respect sells.

## 6. Binge structure (25 min, ~3,700 words at ~150 wpm)
| Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:00 | Cold open | Discovery or last normal moment. End on the central question. |
| 1:00–5:00 | Who they were | Humanise: jobs, hopes, the town. Plant details that pay off later. |
| 5:00–9:00 | The days before | Strange signs, tensions, foreshadowing. Cliffhanger at chapter end. |
| 9:00–13:00 | The night | What is known, through sound, light and place, not gore. Implied, never graphic. |
| 13:00–18:00 | The investigation | Evidence, suspects, mistakes. One twist per 3–4 minutes. |
| 18:00–22:00 | The break / the trial | Turning evidence, arrest or dead end; the courtroom. |
| 22:00–25:00 | Aftermath + open question | Place and people now, remaining theories, respectful close, next-case teaser. |

**Retention devices:** chapter titles every 3–5 min ("Part 3: The Basement") + YouTube chapters • re-hook every 30–90 s •
recurring anchors (house at night, evidence board, map, timeline bar) • pace changes, music drop before key facts • end screen to a related case.

## 7. Ethics and ad-safety (non-negotiable)
- Describe facts, not injuries. First 30 s and thumbnail completely clean.
- Victims are people: names used respectfully, who they were, never joke. No crime-scene photos; illustrated recreations only.
- Unconvicted: "police suspected", "was charged", "was acquitted". Never state guilt beyond the record. Never accuse living people.
- On-screen note: "Illustrated reconstruction based on public records". Never present invented events as true (True Crime Case Files was terminated for AI-invented crimes).
- Never: blood, bodies, weapons pointed at people, real victims' photos, children as victims. No TV-show names in titles.
- Self-certify honestly in Studio's ad questionnaire; request human review if wrongly flagged. 70% evergreen; only material you're allowed to use.

## 8. Visual system (Sections 4 & 5)
**HOUSE STYLE B (paste into every prompt)**
```
- Dark graphic-novel illustration, inked outlines, painterly shading, cinematic
  framing, semi-realistic people (not cartoony), period-accurate clothes and sets.
- Palette: desaturated night blues, stone grey, sepia, olive, ONE warm accent
  (lamp light, red object). Single-source moody lighting, light film grain.
- Violence is implied only: shadows, closed doors, empty rooms, police lights,
  objects, reactions. Never blood, wounds, bodies or weapons in use. 16:9.
```
- Cast 2–6 people per case. Character sheets from documented details only (era, age, hair, clothing); a respectful likeness, never a copied real face. Turnaround → reuse → regenerate drift.
- Scene mix: characters in real places 30% • establishing (house, town, road at night) 20% • object close-up (evidence, letters, door) 15% • map/timeline/evidence board 15% • document (newspaper headline, court record, redrawn) 10% • mood/transition (rain on window, police lights, empty hallway) 10%.
- One image tool with character reference, stay with it. Subtle motion on 5–10% of shots (rain, flicker, smoke); keep characters still.

**Thumbnail (fixed layout):** dark illustrated scene (house at night with one lit window, door ajar, silhouette, lantern or key) + 2–4 words, bold condensed font ("ONE NIGHT", "LOCKED INSIDE") + one red accent.
```
[HOUSE STYLE B]. YouTube thumbnail illustration, 16:9. [SCENE: e.g. a lonely
1910s farmhouse at night in a small Iowa town, one upstairs window glowing warm
yellow, front door slightly open], cold blue moonlight, light fog, one red accent
[e.g. red lantern on the porch]. Strong silhouette, high contrast, simple
composition readable at small size. Keep the LEFT or RIGHT third calm for 2-4 words
of text. No gore, no blood, no bodies, no text in the image.
```
Scene prompt template: same as PDF Section 5 with [HOUSE STYLE B].

## 9. Voice, audio, edit (Section 6)
- Voice: low, calm, measured storyteller; serious, never theatrical (no "Halloween voice"). ~140–150 wpm. Long beats 0.8–1.5 s before key facts. Emphasis: times, places, names, the twist.
- Human narrator strongest. ElevenLabs: a different voice from Channel 1, never changed. Stability 40–55%, Similarity ~75%, Style 15–30%; paragraph by paragraph; check commercial licence. Pronunciation list in `channel-bible/pronunciation.md` (Hinterkaifeck, Villisca, Burari…).
- Music: dark ambient drones, low piano, soft strings; drops out before reveals. SFX very sparse: clock tick, door creak, wind, rain, distant siren.
- Mix: −14 LUFS integrated, true peak < −1 dB • music 20–25 dB under voice, ducked • 0.5–1.5 s silence with music cut before major reveals.
- Edit: new image every 5–9 s • ~200–300 images per 25 min • scale 100→108% very slow, push-ins on faces and doors • hard cuts, 10–15-frame dissolves between chapters, dip to black for big reveals • date/time/place stamps, chapter cards, "Illustrated reconstruction" note • desaturated, film grain, vignette • 1080p/1440p, 24–30 fps.

- Never more than ~7 days without a long video.

## 11. Rules & risks (Section 8)
Inauthentic-content rules (July 2026): no interchangeable template videos, no shock that doesn't pay off. Own research, own-words scripts,
consistent narrator, own art direction, structure that varies with the case, sources in every description. Value test: would a viewer miss this video if it vanished?
Copyright: own AI illustrations or public-domain/openly licensed (National Archives, LoC, Wikimedia with licence). Never Google Images, news photos, others' footage, leaked audio or family photos. Music: Audio Library or licence.
